\begin{abstract}
Gradient descent updates the weights of a factored network, but what it learns is governed by how
the \emph{operator} it computes moves: the input-to-output Jacobian $P(x)$, defined by
$f(x) = P(x)\,x$ for a bias-free MLP. For deep linear networks this induced motion is known
exactly: under balanced initialisation the gain on singular direction $k$ is $L\,s_k^{2-2/L}$, and
the low-rank bias, sequential mode learning and saddle-to-saddle dynamics all follow from that
single exponent. No analogue is known once the gates depend on the input, and the results that do
address nonlinear networks concern endpoints, infinite-depth limits, or ranks rather than
finite-time rates.

We introduce a diagnostic that measures the induced operator motion in any factored network,
validated against the closed form to machine precision, and show that realising a prescribed
operator step in the weights is an exactly solvable problem. Training can therefore be driven
either by gradient descent's step or by the best reachable one, which separates what belongs to
the architecture from what belongs to the update rule.

We report three findings. \textbf{(i)} The gain law survives input-dependent gates: the exponent
rises with depth as $2-2/L$ predicts, but at standard initialisation sits systematically below it,
and does so almost identically for deep linear, CReLU and ReLU networks. An exact decomposition
attributes the entire gap to singular-vector misalignment between the operator and its layer-wise
contexts --- the correction that the balanced theory omits, and which vanishes under balanced
initialisation. \textbf{(ii)} The tilt is a property of the update rule rather than of the
architecture: a step realising the reachable ideal has unit gain on every singular direction.
\textbf{(iii)} The nonlinearity leaves the exponent untouched but suppresses the gain magnitude by
more than an order of magnitude at depth $32$, acting as a stabiliser of the spectral dynamics
rather than a complication.
\end{abstract}
